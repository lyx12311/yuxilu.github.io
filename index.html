About me

Download my CV ADS Google Scholar


I'm a Buckeye Prize Fellow and Ohio State President's Postdoctoral Scholar at the Ohio State University. I obtained an Astronomy Ph.D. from Columbia University in 2023. My dissertation, "Rewinding the Milky Way in Time," explored the history and evolution of our Galaxy through large stellar surveys, with a focus on developing ways to reduce high-dimensional data into physically meaningful and interpretable dimensions. I am interested in stellar physics, Galactic archaeology, and the statistical analysis of exoplanet systems. I specialize in deriving stellar ages from large stellar surveys (e.g., Kepler, TESS, APOGEE, and LAMOST), with a particular focus on gyrochronology calibration. I am also interested in Galactic archaeology, especially radial migration and inferring stellar birth radii. In addition, I am interested in using large surveys to identify unusual or "weird" stars and using these outliers as laboratories for uncovering missing physics in stellar and Galactic evolution..


Exceptional Stars Exceptional Physics:

As mentioned above, I am interested in using large surveys to identify unusual or "weird" stars and using these outliers as laboratories for uncovering missing physics in stellar and Galactic evolution.
Some interesting puzzles are highlighted below. If you have any thoughts or ideas, please reach out and we can solve these mysteries together!
  • Young high-α DWARF stars: Unlike giants, lithium provides a powerful way to rule out past mass-transfer and merger scenarios in dwarf stars. Yet we have found rapidly rotating, high-α dwarf stars that appear to be genuinely young despite their chemically old compositions. Their existence challenges our understanding of Galactic chemical evolution. How did these stars form?
  • Missing metal-rich giants: Where are all the extremely metal-rich giants? We find almost no red giant branch stars beyond [M/H] > 0.4 dex, yet standard stellar models do not predict such a cutoff. Is this real? Roman will tell us!
  • Slow-rotating fully convective stars in the Praesepe and Hyades: Coming soon...

Stellar ages:

My work focuses on deriving stellar ages using gyrochronology and spectroscopy. In particular, I have devoted much of my research to calibrating gyrochronology relations for old, low-mass stars using their kinematics.

Stellar age catalogs I created that are publicly accessible:

  • Gyrochronology relation (GPgyro; available here) to obtain dwarf star ages from 0.67 - 14 Gyr (most accurate >1.5 Gyr) for partially and fully convective stars (~100,000 dwarf stars; available here)
  • Gyro-Kinematic ages (29,949 Kepler ages; available here): We combined kinematic properties (verticle velocity), temperature, magnitude from Gaia, and rotation periods from Kepler to estimate gyro-kinematic ages.
  • Spectroscopic ages and red clump membership (64,317 APOGEE ages: available here): We used The Cannon) to obtain stellar ages and red clump membership from APOGEE spectra. These stellar ages are then used to study the properties of the high- and low-α disks.


Galactic Archaeology:

My work in galactic archaeology has been on analyzing simulations or comparing simulations to Milky Way data to understand the formation history of our own Galaxy. I mainly focus on the importance of radial migration and obtaining birth radii for stars (see one of the figures form the paper explaining the foramtion of the MW).
